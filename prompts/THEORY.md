# THEORY — Key, Harmony, Bass and Melody

The decisions that make a track sound like it was written rather than
generated. Everything here is a choice with a reason you can state.

Related: [GENRES.md](GENRES.md) (genre-typical keys and scales),
[RECIPE.md](RECIPE.md) (where the decisions go), [WORKFLOW.md](WORKFLOW.md)
(phases 4 and 5).

## 1. Key and mode

### Pick the mode for the character, not the scale

| Mode | Character | Typical use |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Natural minor (Aeolian) | direct, dark, unambiguous | trap, dark trap, drill |
| Dorian | dark but hopeful, jazzy without the dominant | lo-fi, boom bap, soul |
| Phrygian | tense, exotic, Middle-Eastern colour | cinematic, dark breaks |
| Mixolydian | dirty, no leading tone | rock, garage, hype |
| Minor pentatonic | no "wrong" notes, instant hook | trap hooks, guitar riffs |
| Major | open, bright | house-pop, cinematic lifts |
| Major 7 | soft, wistful, expensive | lo-fi, chillhop |
| Lydian | floating, filmic | cinematic, ambient |

The trap genre default is **natural minor in a low tonic**, and every
track that does it sounds the same. Two escapes:

1. **Move the tonic** — D minor instead of F minor.
2. **Change the mode** — Dorian or minor pentatonic, keeping the same
   tonic.

### Minor key essentials

- The **♭2** ("flattened second") is the signature tension note in trap
  and dark music. It sits between the tonic and the ♭3.
- The **♭6** and **♭7** are the natural minor palette; the natural 6 and
  7 sound major and fight the mood.
- The **♭5** ("phrygian" note) in a hook is a strong, deliberate choice —
  one per phrase, not a scale.

### Harmonic choices that sound intentional

| Progression | Character |
|-------------|-----------|
| i – VI – III – VII | minor, anthemic, works in every sub-style |
| i – ♭VII – ♭VI – ♭VII | dark trap / metal-leaning |
| i – iv – v | the "sad" version, softer than the major version |
| i – ♭VI – ♭III – ♭VII | pop-trap hybrid |
| i – V – ♭VI – ♭VII | the Andalusian cliché — recognisable, use once |
| i – ♭II – ♭VII – i | phrygian, exotic, cinematic |
| ii – V – i (in major) | the jazz turn, use one per track |
| Pedal point over ♭VI | tension without a chord change |

Two rules:

- **Do not change chords every bar** in a section that should feel
  static. Static is a choice; aimless is not.
- **Do not leave a section on one chord for more than 8 bars** unless it
  is deliberately hypnotic (techno, drone) — and even then, change one
  other parameter.

## 2. Bass writing

The bass is not "the kick, lower". It has its own job.

### Three bass roles

| Role | What it does | How |
|------|--------------|-----|
| **Foundation** | supports the harmony | root notes, long, in the low octave |
| **Motion** | carries the groove | rhythmic, syncopated, following the drums |
| **Hook** | is the memorable line | a melodic cell you could hum |

Pick one per track. A bass doing all three at once is muddy.

### Writing rules

- **Pitch before rhythm.** A bassline with only on/off is a kick. Give it
  a contour: root → ♭5 → root, or root → ♭7 → ♭3.
- **Lock or answer.** Either it doubles the kick exactly (foundation) or
  it answers it with a delay (motion). Half-locked is a mistake.
- **The root is not always the tonic.** A bass that only plays the root
  of the chord is a pad.
- **Range discipline.** 808s belong around 40–80 Hz; a melodic bass
  belongs around 80–200 Hz. Two basses in one register fight.
- **The slide is the cheapest 808 trick**: slide into the downbeat of
  every 4th bar (or every 8 bars in a slower track).
- **End on the root.** The last note before a section change should be
  the tonic or the 5th, so the next section lands.

## 3. Melody writing

### A melody is a phrase

- **Contour**: a melody should go somewhere. Most melodies are an arch,
  a descent, or an arch with a raised peak.
- **Range**: 5–7 scale steps is enough for a hook. Wider is a statement,
  not a default.
- **Rhythm**: the rhythm carries the hook more than the notes do. A
  memorable melody on simple pitches beats a forgettable one on a wide
  range.
- **Phrasing**: leave a rest where the phrase needs air. Silence between
  notes is what makes a line feel sung rather than played.
- **Repetition with variation**: the hook repeats, but each return moves
  — transposed, rhythmically displaced, doubled, or answered.

### Avoiding the generated-melody sound

| Tells | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Every note is the same length | vary the durations |
| The melody never leaves the scale's centre | reach for the 4th or ♭6 as a passing note |
| The contour is a straight line | break it once per phrase |
| It follows the kick exactly | place notes *between* the kick hits |
| No dynamics | vary velocity 15–25% |
| It resolves predictably | end on the 5th or the ♭7 instead of the tonic once |

### One deliberate flaw

Per track, one of:

- a note a quarter-tone flat or sharp,
- a note a 32nd early,
- a note that breaks the swing,
- a velocity that jumps for no reason,
- a note that skips the expected scale step.

One. Two read as a mistake.

## 4. Harmony in practice

- **Decide the chord for the section, then the bass note under it.**
  Write them together; a bass note that is not in the chord is a
  deliberate dissonance and must sound like one.
- **The hook's harmony should differ from the verse's.** Same harmony,
  same melody, louder = boring.
- **When you cannot name the chord, do not write it.** Use the root and
  the 5th — it is always safe, and it is what most of the genre uses.
- **Layer the 3rd only when it matters.** The 3rd decides major vs minor;
  leaving it out is a legitimate, very common production choice.

## 5. The theory gate

- [ ] The key and mode are stated, and the reason is a reason (mood,
      reference, existing sample) — not "it sounds good".
- [ ] Every section has a defined harmonic centre, or is deliberately
      static.
- [ ] The bass has one defined role and a contour, not only on/off hits.
- [ ] The hook is hummable with the drums removed.
- [ ] At least one deliberate imperfection exists, and only one.
- [ ] The melody's range and rhythm vary — it is not a scale walk.
